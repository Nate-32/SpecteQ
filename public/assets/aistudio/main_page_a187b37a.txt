SPECTEQ 2027 — MAIN WEBSITE PAGE SPECIFICATION Version 1.0 | Reference
document for website-building agent

PURPOSE This document defines the Main Page of the SpecteQ 2027 website:
structure, content, visual direction, interactions, and factual-data
rules. It is a specification, not an implementation prompt.

IMPORTANT PAGE ARCHITECTURE The website has separate pages: 1. MAIN
PAGE: one long scrolling landing page, containing all sections below. 2.
GALLERY PAGE: a completely separate page/route. The previous-year
photo/video gallery must NOT be embedded into the Main Page. Main
navigation and footer may link to Gallery.

MAIN PAGE SECTION ORDER 1. Hero 2. What Is SpecteQ? 3. Explore the
Competition — 3 Categories 4. Category Comparison / Choose Your Mission
5. Participant Journey 6. Event Progression / Level System 7.
Technologies to Learn 8. Ready to Choose CTA 9. Event Timeline 10.
Prizes & Rewards 11. Rules & Guidelines 12. FAQ 13. Registration —
Insert Coin / Join the Game 14. Footer — Quick Links & Connect

VISUAL THEME Modern retro arcade / game-launcher interface. Cinematic
neon coastal city and waterfront at dusk, modern skyline, reflections,
atmospheric sunset, subtle palm silhouettes. Premium dark UI,
translucent glass panels, thin neon borders, restrained glow, gradients,
grid/HUD details, numbered labels, status chips, progress bars, subtle
micro-animation. Palette: deep navy/near-black/dark purple, cyan,
magenta, violet, orange accents, white/light-grey text. Do NOT use
copyrighted GTA/Vice City logos, characters, branding, or assets. No
human characters in hero background. Do not make it old-fashioned 8-bit
or cluttered cyberpunk. Keep it modern, polished, readable, and
responsive.

TYPOGRAPHY Hero title: large, wide, bold, futuristic italic display type
similar to reference. Section headings: futuristic geometric display
font. Body: clean modern sans-serif. HUD/metadata: monospaced or
technical font. Slogan may use expressive/script-like gradient
lettering. Limit font families and maintain consistent hierarchy.

NAVIGATION Sticky translucent navigation with SpecteQ wordmark and
links: Home, About, Missions/Categories, Journey, Technologies,
Timeline, Rewards, Rules, FAQ, Gallery, Registration/Join Game.
Main-page links smooth-scroll. Gallery navigates to its separate
page/route. Registration CTA scrolls to registration section. Mobile
navigation must be compact and functional. Highlight current
section/page where practical.

SECTION 01 — HERO Background: full-width cinematic sunset
city/waterfront with dark overlay for contrast; no people. Text: Small
label: “THE BOT COMPETITION · 2027 EDITION” Main title: “SpecteQ 2027”
Slogan: “Build. Battle. Dominate.” Description: “Enter the ultimate
robotics arena. Learn, experiment, engineer your machines, and compete
to achieve the highest score.” Primary CTA: “Enter the Arena →” — scroll
to registration section. Secondary CTA: “Explore Missions” — scroll to

Open Entry STATUS: READY | CURRENT LEVEL: 00 / 03 | MISSION STATUS: NOT
STARTED This is illustrative UI only. Do not imply the visitor is logged
in or that real progress is tracked. Cyan READY, white level, orange NOT

responsively.

SECTION 02 — WHAT IS SPECTEQ? Title: “WHAT IS SPECTEQ?” Subtitle: “An
inclusive robotics competition for all skill levels.” Suggested intro:
“SpecteQ is a hands-on robotics competition where students learn, build,
experiment, and compete. Participants move through training, robot
development, testing, and arena challenges—turning concepts into working
machines.” Three feature cards: A. COLLEGE COMPETITION — Students come
together to compete, collaborate, and showcase engineering skills. B.
ROBOTICS COMPETITION — Experience robotics through practical challenges,
problem-solving, and competition in a learning-while-competing
environment. C. HANDS-ON LEARNING — Explore robotics through practical
challenges, guided learning, experimentation, and real-world engineering
experience. Use icons, relevant images/illustrations, or a configurable
launch-film media panel. Do not show a fake video player if no media
asset exists.

SECTION 03 — EXPLORE THE COMPETITION / THREE CATEGORIES Title: “EXPLORE
THE COMPETITION” Subtitle: “Choose your challenge. Build your machine.
Enter the arena.” Show exactly these current categories, based on the
white website reference: 1. LINE FOLLOWER — Build a robot that follows a
designated path using sensors, precise navigation, and control
algorithms. 2. MAZE SOLVER — Create an intelligent robot that navigates
a maze, detects obstacles/dead ends, and finds its way toward the goal.
3. SELF BALANCING — Design a two-wheeled robot that maintains balance
using sensors, feedback control, and real-time decision-making. Each
card: large category-specific robot visual, category number/mission
code, title, short description, optional difficulty/technology tags,
“Explore Mission” CTA. Cards may open details or expand. Avoid unrelated
generic robot imagery. Exact rules, specifications, track layouts, and
scoring remain configurable until officially confirmed.

SECTION 04 — CATEGORY COMPARISON / CHOOSE YOUR MISSION Title: “CHOOSE
YOUR MISSION” Subtitle: “Every mission demands a different way of
thinking.” Compare: LINE FOLLOWER Core: Follow a designated path
accurately and efficiently. Skills: IR sensors, calibration, motor
control, PID/control tuning, path tracking. Learning: Sensor
calibration, accuracy, speed optimisation, control systems. Difficulty:
Beginner–Intermediate (confirm officially). Ideal: Beginners or teams
interested in embedded control. MAZE SOLVER Core: Navigate a maze,
handle obstacles/dead ends, find a route. Skills: Sensors, navigation,
path planning, algorithm design. Learning: Algorithm design, sensor
integration, decision-making. Difficulty: Intermediate (confirm
officially). Ideal: Teams interested in programming, logic, autonomous
navigation. SELF BALANCING Core: Maintain a two-wheeled robot upright
while balancing/moving. Skills: IMU/gyroscope, control theory, PID
tuning, filtering, motor control. Learning: Control theory, signal
processing, system dynamics. Difficulty: Advanced (confirm officially).
Ideal: Experienced teams seeking a more advanced control challenge. Use
responsive comparison cards or a table that becomes stacked cards on
mobile. Highlight differences and provide “Choose this Mission” action.
Do not imply mission choice is final unless confirmed.

SECTION 05 — PARTICIPANT JOURNEY Title: “YOUR JOURNEY” Subtitle: “From
first boot to High Score Champion.” Terminology: Participants = Players;
Teams = Gaming Teams; Workshops = Missions; Learning stages = Levels;
Project completion = Mission Complete; Final competition = The Robotics
Arena; Winners = High Score Champions. Stages: LEVEL 00 — INSERT COIN:
Registration/onboarding. “Create your Player profile, form your Gaming
Team, choose your mission track, and prepare to enter SpecteQ.” LEVEL 01
— TRAINING: Workshops/learning. “Learn the foundations, explore
essential technologies, and prepare for your robotics challenge.” LEVEL
02 — BUILD: Robot development. “Design, assemble, program, and improve
your robot.” LEVEL 03 — TEST: Testing/debugging/calibration. “Test your
robot, refine performance, debug systems, and prepare for the arena.”
FINAL — THE ROBOTICS ARENA: “Take your machine to the arena, compete,
complete the challenge, and aim for the High Score.” Desktop: horizontal
journey with connecting line. Mobile: vertical or swipeable. Stages
selectable; selection may reveal detail. Keep the real-world meaning
clear.

SECTION 07 — TECHNOLOGIES TO LEARN Title: “TECHNOLOGIES TO LEARN”
Subtitle: “Master essential robotics skills.” Use the two supplied
technology-section screenshots as inspiration for an animated/moving
layout—e.g. subtle marquee, staggered moving card columns, carousel, or
gentle scrolling motion. This is not a pixel-for-pixel copy. Keep motion
smooth, readable, responsive, and respect reduced-motion settings.
Technology groups: EMBEDDED SYSTEMS: Arduino/microcontrollers, motor
controllers, sensor integration, PWM control. PROGRAMMING: C/C++,
algorithm design, real-time control, embedded programming. CONTROL
SYSTEMS: PID controllers, feedback loops, sensor fusion, balance
control. NAVIGATION: path planning, maze solving, line following,
obstacle detection. SENSORS: IR sensors, ultrasonic sensors,
gyroscope/accelerometer, calibration. ELECTRONICS: circuit design, power
management, motor drivers, signal processing. These are learning
opportunities, not guarantees every participant will cover every item.
